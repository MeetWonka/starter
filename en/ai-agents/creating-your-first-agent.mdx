Create a specialized AI agent tailored to your workflow. This guide walks through the guided creation flow, from your first idea to a shared, working agent.

<Info>
Building an agent means defining its identity, writing instructions, choosing a model size, and optionally connecting tools.
</Info>

## Starting an Agent

<Steps>
<Step title="Open New Agent">
In the sidebar, open **Agents** → **New agent**. You land in a guided creation flow, in the main conversation area (there's no separate side panel to look for).
</Step>

<Step title="Start from Scratch or a Template">
Choose to start blank, or click **Browse templates** to start from a ready-made template and customize it. Templates are a fast way to see a working example before writing your own instructions from scratch.
</Step>
</Steps>

## The Guided Creation Wizard

The wizard walks you through five steps:

<Steps>
<Step title="Identity">
Choose a clear, descriptive name and a short description.

**Good:** "Customer Support Assistant," "Q4 Sales Data Analyzer"
**Avoid:** "Helper," "Agent 1"

<Tip>
If you plan to share the agent with your organization, pick a name that makes sense to everyone, not just to you.
</Tip>
</Step>

<Step title="Model">
Pick a model size for your agent. WonkaChat groups models as fast, standard, or advanced rather than surfacing every provider by name, and each model shows a region badge indicating where it runs.

- **Fast** models suit simple, high-volume tasks (data lookup, formatting, quick replies)
- **Standard** models are a good default for most agents
- **Advanced** models suit complex, multi-step reasoning, coding, or planning

<Info>
For a full comparison of model sizes and when to use each, see [Choosing a Model](/en/improved-usage/choosing-a-model).
</Info>
</Step>

<Step title="Instructions">
Write your agent's instructions: its role, tone, operational rules, and any business context it needs.

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Role Definition">
State what the agent is and does.

**Example:** "You are a customer support specialist for our SaaS platform. Help users resolve technical issues, answer product questions, and guide them through common workflows."
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Communication Style">
Define tone and format.

**Example:** "Communicate in a friendly, professional tone. Be concise but thorough. Use bullet points for clarity."
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Operational Guidelines">
Provide specific rules for how the agent should execute tasks and make decisions.

**Example:** "When analyzing sales data, always compare against the previous quarter. If data is incomplete, note the gaps explicitly rather than making assumptions."
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Business Context">
Include company-specific information.

**Example:** "Support hours: 9 AM-6 PM. We offer three tiers: Basic, Pro, Enterprise."
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Check>
Clear, detailed instructions are the foundation of agent effectiveness. See [Business Teaching](/en/ai-agents/business-teaching) for more on this.
</Check>
</Step>

<Step title="Capabilities">
Turn on any capabilities your agent needs:

- **Code Execution** — if enabled for your organization
- **Web Search** — if enabled for your organization, toggle **Allow searching the web** for this agent
- **Connectors** — add the connectors (MCP) the agent should use

<Warning>
Agents only access connectors explicitly assigned to them, and inherit your personal permissions on those systems. See [Access Control](/en/security-governance/access-control) for details.
</Warning>

**Tool selection principles:**
- Only add connectors the agent actually needs
- Too many connectors can cause selection confusion
- Make sure you personally have permission for any connector you assign
</Step>

<Step title="Review">
Review your configuration, test it with a few real requests, and create the agent.
</Step>
</Steps>

## After Creation: Audit and Share

Once the agent is created, the flow moves into two more stages.

<Steps>
<Step title="Audit">
Review the agent's activity and configuration before putting it in front of others. This is a good time to test edge cases and confirm the agent behaves as expected.

<Info>
See [Audit Your Agents](/en/ai-agents/audit-your-agents) for details.
</Info>
</Step>

<Step title="Share">
Decide who else should be able to use the agent.

- **Personal use**: keep it private, it's ready to use immediately
- **Share with specific people or groups**: as Viewer, Editor, or Owner
- **Share with everyone in your organization**: makes the agent available to every member

<Info>
Whether you can share agents — and with everyone in your organization — depends on your role's permissions and your organization's settings. If sharing options are missing, ask your administrator.

See [Sharing Agents](/en/ai-agents/sharing-agents) for the full walkthrough.
</Info>
</Step>
</Steps>

## Common Mistakes

<AccordionGroup>
<Accordion title="Vague Instructions">
**Problem:** "You are a helpful assistant" lacks guidance.
**Solution:** Be specific about role, expertise, tone, and guidelines.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Too Many Responsibilities">
**Problem:** One agent handling unrelated tasks like customer support and data analysis.
**Solution:** Create focused agents for specific workflows.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Connector Overload">
**Problem:** Assigning every available connector.
**Solution:** Only add the connectors the agent needs, to avoid selection confusion.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Insufficient Testing">
**Problem:** Sharing an agent without thorough testing.
**Solution:** Test extensively with realistic scenarios during the Audit stage first.
</Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Security Best Practices

### Limit Connector Access

Only grant the connectors that are strictly necessary for the agent's task. If your agent drafts emails, consider leaving out a "send email" capability so you keep final control over sending.

### Isolate Integrations

Avoid connecting systems that shouldn't interact with each other in the same agent. Create separate agents for different domains to maintain clear boundaries.

### Test Iteratively

Begin with minimal permissions and test extensively before expanding capabilities.

**Recommended approach:**
- Start with read-only access when possible
- Test with non-production data first
- Gradually add connectors as you verify behavior

<Info>
See [Security & Governance](/en/security-governance/security-overview) for comprehensive security practices.
</Info>
